# Unit 3: A Map, a Shorter Timeline, and a Clearer Power Sort

Requested by the teacher on Nov 14: check the timeline and the leaderboard's menus; the
leaderboard has a map run time, so give Unit 3 a good map, with fewer places to click than last
year's states and territories map and with features such as the Mississippi River; cut the
timeline to about ten key events and suggest a better approach; make Power Sort more engaging,
with larger text and symbols or colour for contrast.

Power Sort is being restyled now, since the request is specific. The map and the timeline wait
for the teacher's answers below.

## Findings

### The leaderboard and maps

- The students' leaderboard always shows four tabs: Top Students, **Map Speed Run**, Class
  Battle, Achievements. Unit 3 has no map, so its Map Speed Run tab is empty, and Unit 3
  students cannot earn the map bonus (100 points for a perfect run, plus up to 180 for speed)
  that Unit 2 students could.
- The site already has the pieces for a new map:
  - a **50 States** map with a learn mode and a quiz mode;
  - last year's **Map Quiz** of states and territories, used by two hidden units;
  - Unit 2's **Colonial Geography** map, drawn on a base map of the eastern United States that
    already holds the Mississippi and Ohio rivers, the Great Lakes, the Appalachian Mountains,
    the Atlantic, the Gulf of Mexico, and cities including Philadelphia and New York.
- The reference map the teacher linked (geoguessr.com/vgp/3620) could not be opened from the
  build session, so its exact contents were not seen.

### The timeline

- Unit 3's timeline has **19 events** in one list, to be placed in 19 numbered slots. On a
  Chromebook the screen shows about ten of them; the rest need scrolling.
- Three of the events fall within five months of 1787, and two more within a year of each
  other in 1794 and 1795, so much of the task is telling months apart.
- The activity checks only the order. It does not ask why one event led to another.

### Power Sort

- The statement to sort is 21 px, the bin names 14 px, and the hint under each name 11 px.
- The three bins are the same dark blue as each other and close to the background, with a
  faint border, and carry no symbol.

## Timeline Redesign: Story Threads (proposed, not built)

The ten core events are split into three **threads**. Each thread is one short chain in which
every event led to the next. Students put a thread in order by asking "what had to happen
first?", with the years hidden. When a thread is right, a line appears between each pair saying
why one led to the other.

Because every link in a thread is a real cause, its order and its dates always agree, so there is
no conflict between "logical" and "correct". The threads overlap in time: the Northwest Ordinance
passed while the Convention was meeting. A last screen lays all three threads along one line of
years, so students see that history runs in several stories at once.

### The threads and the lines between events (drafts for the teacher)

**1. Why a new government? (3 events)**

- Articles of Confederation take effect, 1781 → Shays' Rebellion begins, 1786:
  "Under the Articles, Congress could not tax, so the states raised their own taxes to pay war
  debts. In Massachusetts, farmers who could not pay those taxes rose up."
- Shays' Rebellion → Constitutional Convention opens in Philadelphia, 1787:
  "Congress had no money to send an army to stop the rebellion. Many leaders decided the Articles
  had to change, and delegates met in Philadelphia in May 1787."

**2. From plan to government (4 events)**

- Delegates sign the Constitution, 1787 → New Hampshire becomes the ninth state to ratify, 1788:
  "The Constitution would take effect once nine states ratified it, so each state held a
  convention to vote on it."
- New Hampshire, ninth state → George Washington becomes the first president, 1789:
  "With nine states in, the new government could begin. Electors chose Washington, and he took
  office in April 1789."
- Washington, first president → Bill of Rights is ratified, 1791:
  "Several states ratified only after being promised a list of rights. The first Congress wrote
  the amendments in 1789, and the states ratified ten of them by 1791."

**3. Land, treaties and the supreme law (3 events)**

- Northwest Ordinance passes, 1787 → Treaty of Medicine Creek, 1854:
  "The Ordinance promised that Native land would not be taken without consent, so the United
  States gained land by treaty. As it spread to the Pacific it kept making treaties, and in 1854
  it signed the Treaty of Medicine Creek with the Nisqually, Puyallup and other nations."
  (This link is a pattern more than a direct cause; the teacher should judge it.)
- Treaty of Medicine Creek → Boldt decision, 1974:
  "The treaty promised the right to fish at 'usual and accustomed' places. When Washington State
  arrested Native fishers, the tribes went to court, and in 1974 Judge George Boldt ruled the
  treaty was the supreme law of the land."

### Other choices

- **A wrong order:** the first link that is out of place is marked, with a nudge ("Could this
  happen before that? Read both cards again."), and the student tries again. No penalty.
- **The years:** hidden while ordering; a "Show years" button stays as help, and every year
  appears on the closing screen.
- **The nine other events:** drop them from the timeline, or keep them as a "Full timeline"
  bonus, ordered by date with years shown, for students who want more.

### Decisions for the teacher

1. Use story threads?
2. Are the three threads right?
3. Approve, or edit, the seven lines.
4. How a wrong order is handled.
5. What happens to the nine other events.

Size: medium. The timeline activity is rebuilt for threads; the events and lines live in the
unit's config, so other units keep the old timeline until they get threads of their own.

## Story Threads, Round 2: Clearer Links (proposed Nov 14, not built)

Threads shipped locally as 8.55.124 (not pushed). Reviewing it, the teacher wrote: "some of the
connections won't be clear to 8th graders... How can we improve this?"

### Findings, link by link

| Link | Problem |
|---|---|
| Articles → Shays' Rebellion | Skips a step: Congress could not tax, so the states taxed their people hard. That step is on neither card. |
| Shays' → Convention | Clear: the Shays' card says Congress had no army to send. |
| Signing → ninth state | Clear: the signing card says nine states must ratify. |
| Ninth state → Washington | "Electors" is never explained. |
| Washington → Bill of Rights | Not a cause. The cause is the promise made to win ratification. |
| Northwest Ordinance → Medicine Creek | A pattern 67 years apart, not a cause. |
| Medicine Creek → Boldt | Skips 120 years; the arrests (the Fish Wars) are on no card. |

### Proposals

1. **Add the missing step as its own card** where a link jumps: "States raise taxes to pay war
   debts (1780s)" in thread 1, and "Native fishers are arrested in the Fish Wars (1960s)" in
   thread 3. Students then place the step themselves instead of having to imagine it.
2. **Thread 3 rebuilt** as Northwest Ordinance → Treaty of Greenville (already an event) →
   Treaty of Medicine Creek → Fish Wars → Boldt decision. The Ordinance-to-Greenville link is a
   real cause (settlers moved in, war, a treaty). The Greenville-to-Medicine Creek link is a
   pattern, so it asks "What pattern repeats?" instead of "What connects these?"
3. **Rewrite every link in plain words:** the choice reads as a short "because" phrase, and the
   why line is two short sentences that explain any hard word (ratify, electors) in place.
4. **A "Need a hint?" button** on each link that points to the clue on the cards ("Look at what
   the Articles card says Congress could not do").

Thread 2 keeps its four events; its last link is reworded around the real cause, the promise.
Size: small to medium; mostly new wording, plus the hint button and the pattern question.
